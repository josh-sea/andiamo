#2 US 10Y Yield at 3-Year High — Treasury Buyback 'Backfire' ⚡ 88 Fixed Income / Macro

What is happening: 10Y Treasury yields hit 4.85% — the highest since late 2023. Treasury Secretary Bessent announced a $6B bond buyback operation explicitly to suppress long yields. The market was disappointed (expected $6–8B), and yields ROSE after the announcement. The 20Y and 30Y moved up sharply as well. The dollar fell to a 7-month low vs. yen. US national debt has crossed $40 trillion.

Why it matters: A failed bond intervention is a qualitatively different signal than just rising yields. It tells you: (1) the market does not believe the Treasury can materially move the needle at current buyback sizes, (2) the fiscal premium is real and re-pricing, (3) the 'Bessent Put' is not credible at these levels. QuantStreet's September note flagged 'global treasury jitters and rising long-term interest rates' as the dominant market risk in August. Oxford Economics explicitly said 'you can't push the bond market around.'

What to watch: UBS now forecasts TWO Fed hikes in 2026 under Warsh. Prediction markets at 57–58% for a September hike. August CPI is the decisive data point. Watch for Treasury to announce a much larger buyback or shift strategy.

Key question: Is the yield spike an oil/inflation scare that fades post-CPI, or is it a structural fiscal credibility problem that persists regardless of CPI?

Prior parallels: UK LDI/Gilt crisis Oct 2022 (intervention failed initially). Japan YCC collapse 2023. Both showed that bond market can overwhelm intervention when the underlying fiscal/monetary signal is wrong.

Flags:
