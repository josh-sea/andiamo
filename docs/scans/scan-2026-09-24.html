#1 The Stagflation Setup Is Real — Fed Hiked Into a War Economy ⚡ 88 MACRO / RATES

What is happening: The Fed raised rates 25bps last week (to 3.75–4.00%) — its first hike in three years — while the US-Iran war keeps oil above $100/bbl and Strait of Hormuz disruptions persist. The Fed's own SEP signals another hike this year. Simultaneously, consumer spending is flagging and the Yahoo Finance 'double-whammy' narrative is emerging: slowing demand + tightening policy simultaneously.

Why it matters: This is the textbook stagflation input. The Fed is hiking into a supply shock, not a demand shock. That makes the policy error risk high — tightening into slowing growth will hit mid/small caps, housing, and credit harder than mega-cap tech. The Seeking Alpha piece specifically flags mega-caps masking real economy stress.

Key tension: Bond markets are bombing fresh (Reuters headline: 'Bonds bomb'). 10Y UST reportedly hit 5.11% — highest since 2007. That level historically causes forced de-risking in levered positions, pension fund rebalancing, and mortgage market seizure.

Base rate note: Stagflation scenarios occurred in ~3 of last 12 Fed tightening cycles. But rate hike INTO active war with oil > $100 is extremely rare — essentially 1973-analog.
