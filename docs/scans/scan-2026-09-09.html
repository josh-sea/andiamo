#1 The Great Bond Selloff of 2026: Term Premium, Not Warsh ⚡ 92 MACRO — RATES

What is happening: 10-year Treasury yields have risen ~20bps since the July FOMC, approaching 4.8%, with the 30-year up similarly. Crucially, JPMorgan's sign-restriction model attributes the move overwhelmingly to rising term premium and economic demand — NOT hawkish monetary policy. The Economist, Morningstar, and the NYT all published major pieces this week. The Treasury responded on Sept 9 by announcing $6B in long-bond buybacks (Bessent's 'doubling' promise), which paradoxically sent yields *higher*, suggesting the market reads it as insufficient or as a signal of distress.

Key tension: The narrative war: Bessent/bulls say it's AI productivity optimism pulling capital forward. Bears say it's fiscal deficit fear (Trump tax cuts + $1T+ AI capex with no FCF) plus Middle East tail risk. JPM's data suggests the bears have it right — term premium is the driver, not growth expectations.

What to watch: Sept 16 FOMC decision. CPI data this week. Whether the $6B buyback actually moves the needle or gets swallowed. 30-year auction dynamics.

Investable angle: Long short-duration Treasuries (market already doing this per ETF flow data); short long-duration (TLT); utilities sector as contrarian oversold setup once yields peak. Seek alpha via refinery names if energy-bond correlation thesis holds.

Risks: If Warsh holds and hike materializes, term premium could compress quickly. The move could be a 1994 analog — 'normalization shock' that resolves without recession.

#2 Refined Products as the Hidden Inflation Channel: Diesel & Jet Fuel Near Records ⚡ 87 COMMODITY / MACRO — ENERGY

What is happening: While crude oil ($80–$100 Brent range) and gasoline headlines dominate, the real pressure is in diesel (+50% since Iran conflict began) and jet fuel (+60%). NYT ran a dedicated piece Aug 26. The Strait of Hormuz collapse (from 21M bpd to as low as 2M bpd at peak disruption) hit heavy crude flows harder — US refineries built for heavy crude are at max capacity processing light shale oil, creating a structural bottleneck that cannot be resolved by simply pumping more domestic crude. Singapore bunker prices +76% since the war began. Russia simultaneously halted diesel exports in July.

Key tension: This is an analog to 1973 refined products crisis, not a spot-crude crisis. The market is pricing oil; it is not fully pricing the downstream crack spread and refining margin story. JPMorgan explicitly flagged this as the bond-yield synergy mechanism — rising diesel/jet fuel feeds CPI via airfares, trucking, agriculture, and manufacturing input costs.

What to watch: Weekly EIA refined product inventory data. Crack spreads (3-2-1). Any Strait of Hormuz reopening news. Russia diesel export policy reversal.

Investable angle: US domestic refinery names with heavy-crude conversion capacity are potentially squeezed. Pure-play diesel/distillate exposure. Airline sector short (cost pressure without commensurate airfare pricing power). Agricultural inputs (fertilizer, farm machinery operating costs).

Risks: A ceasefire or Hormuz reopening could normalize spreads rapidly. This thesis is entirely contingent on the geopolitical durability of the disruption.

#4 Robinhood Stock Tokens vs. AMC: Offshore Synthetic Equity and the SEC Perimeter Battle ⚡ 74 REGULATORY / FINTECH / STRUCTURAL

What is happening: AMC CEO Adam Aron publicly attacked Robinhood on Sept 3 for issuing Jersey-domiciled tokenized debt securities tracking AMC stock without issuer consent. Robinhood ($194 tokens, $140M in assets as of Sept 9) refused to stop, backed by Dan Gallagher (former SEC Commissioner). The tokens are: (1) structured as limited-recourse debt vs. a Jersey entity, (2) backed 1:1 by actual underlying equity held at Alpaca Securities, (3) governed by Swiss law, passported through Liechtenstein into 29 EEA states, (4) explicitly unsupervised by any jurisdiction per their own prospectus. AMC token supply grew 850% in 18 hours after the controversy. No EDGAR filing containing 'stock tokens' existed before Sept 1 — now several have appeared.

Key tension: This is NOT a novel legal argument — it is the CFD model that IG Group, Plus500, CMC have run for 20 years. The novel elements are: (a) ERC-20 wrapper making it transferable peer-to-peer without broker intermediation, (b) retail-first global distribution at scale, (c) explicit 1:1 backing making it arguably stronger than a naked CFD. The real question is where the SEC draws the perimeter — and whether this is a Robinhood-specific fight or an industry-wide regulatory moment.

What to watch: SEC response / formal comment on SR-NASDAQ-2025-072 (Nasdaq filed this issue a year ago). Whether other issuers join AMC in complaining. Robinhood's next EDGAR filing. Any injunctive filing.

Investable angle: Robinhood (HOOD) — if SEC validates the model, this is a massive distribution moat for tokenized equity globally. If SEC clamps down, a regulatory liability. Binary event setup. Short-term: watch if other retail brokers announce competing token products (competitive legitimization).

Risks: Regulatory risk is highly asymmetric and timeline-uncertain. AMC itself is financially fragile — its objection may be more about price manipulation optics than legal substance. The 850% token supply surge suggests speculative interest, not legitimate hedging demand.
