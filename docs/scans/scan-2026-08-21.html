#1 Broadcom's $60–100B AI Debt Mega-Structure: Shadow Credit Goes Mainstream ⚡ 92 AI Infrastructure / Private Credit / Structural Finance

Summary: Broadcom is in talks to raise $60–100B in debt via special-purpose vehicles to finance AI chips for Anthropic and others. Apollo and Blackstone are the key lenders. The structure mirrors the $35B 'AI XPV' deal closed in June — this is its sequel, potentially 3x larger. Senior tranches (~$60–70B) would be backstopped by Broadcom guarantees to achieve investment-grade ratings. A junior tranche (~$30B) sits below. The stated partnership target is 20 gigawatts of compute by 2028, costing 'hundreds of billions.' Only 1/20th is financed so far.

Why it matters: This is the clearest evidence yet that AI infrastructure is being financed like real estate — off-balance-sheet SPVs, senior/junior tranches, institutional backstops. The SEC just ruled data-center securitization sits outside Dodd-Frank risk retention rules, greenlighting more of this. Broadcom CEO Hock Tan guided >$100B in AI chip revenue for 2027. The structure concentrates credit risk on Broadcom (guarantor) and Apollo/Blackstone (holders). If AI revenue disappoints, these vehicles unwind badly. The bull case: investment-grade ratings mean pension/insurance capital floods in, sustainably lowering cost of AI buildout.

Key questions:
  • What is Broadcom's actual contingent liability if Anthropic revenue disappoints? Bloomberg counted $70B of 'shadow credit backstops' across AI companies as of Aug 15.
  • Is this circular? Burry claims yes — Nvidia/Broadcom finance the buyers of their own chips. Huang denies it. The truth matters for earnings quality.
  • How correlated are Apollo and Blackstone's exposures across multiple AI SPVs?

#2 The Great Bond Repricing: US 30yr at 5.33%, Japan at 4.1%, UK Gilts at 5.86% ⚡ 88 Macro / Fixed Income / Global Capital Competition

The cause is NOT primarily inflation — it's a structural competition for capital: (1) governments running large deficits and issuing massive debt supply, (2) AI capex turning big tech into mega-borrowers, (3) Japan normalizing rates, reducing the pool of capital chasing yield overseas, (4) European defense spending surge. TLT (long-duration ETF) behavior here is the clearest macro signal in the market.

  • Does AI capex demand (Broadcom/Nvidia SPV financing) structurally crowd out government borrowing in private credit markets?
  • What is the breakeven yield level that begins to visibly compress S&P P/E multiples?

His core claim: Nvidia and Big Tech are funding the very customers that buy their chips, via SPVs and private credit, which artificially inflates Nvidia's top-line revenue and masks true end-demand. This is the most important question in tech investing right now. Bloomberg independently flagged ~$70B of 'shadow credit backstops' as of Aug 15. The CDS spike is a real market signal.

Base rate check: Short theses on high-momentum tech names have a poor win rate over 1-2yr horizons but a better record over 3-5yr. Burry's timing on the housing crisis was early by ~2 years. The Enron comparison will only be validated if AI revenue growth decelerates sharply while debt loads remain — not yet in the data.
