#1 Fed Resumes Rate Hikes Under Warsh — First Increase in 3+ Years, Into a Pre-Midterm Window ⚡ 92 Macro / Rates

What is happening: Chairman Kevin Warsh is expected to deliver a 25bps hike Wednesday (Sept 16-17 FOMC), the first rate increase since 2023. Markets are 87-90% priced for it. Inflation remains above target; Warsh struck a hawkish tone at Jackson Hole. Critically, this hike comes 7 weeks before midterm elections — creating direct friction with the Trump administration. Trump has publicly pledged to 'respect' the decision, but White House frustration is reported. 30-year Treasury yields are at multi-decade highs; the Treasury has launched a double-buyback program to cap long-end pressure.

Why interesting: A Fed hike INTO rising oil prices ($107-$110 Brent), INTO a bond market already at multi-decade yield highs, and INTO a midterm cycle is textbook late-cycle stagflation risk. The political overlay (Warsh vs. Trump) adds institutional credibility risk. Equity markets have so far shrugged ('no sign of panic'), which itself is a signal — either complacency or genuine regime resilience. Need to determine which.

Bear case flag: Equity markets pricing a soft landing despite hike + oil + rates. If history rhymes with 1973 or 2022, complacency here is dangerous.

Amundi notes EM resilience YTD (+26% EM vs. -8% MSCI China), but flags FX as the principal vulnerability. Meanwhile, a US-BoJ joint currency intervention reinforced the 'dollar debasement trade.'

Why interesting: The dollar is being pulled in two directions simultaneously: hawkish Fed (dollar positive) vs. Treasury buyback + debasement trade + BRICS dedollarization momentum (dollar negative). EM currencies in oil-importing nations face the worst of both worlds — high oil import costs AND dollar strength. The BoJ regime change (4 hikes expected before mid-2027, rate to 2%) is the most underappreciated global fixed income risk in the dataset. A disorderly yen adjustment could spike global bond volatility in a way that dwarfs the Fed hike.

Bear case flag: EM resilience has been repeatedly predicted to crack and repeatedly hasn't. The 26% YTD EM outperformance cuts both ways — could be late-cycle euphoria OR structural rerating.
