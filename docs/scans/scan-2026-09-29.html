#2 Hormuz Ceasefire Hanging by a Thread: Qatar Mediating as of Sept 29 — Potential 15-20% Oil Shock Optionality ⚡ 88 Geopolitical Macro / Cross-Asset Event Risk

What is happening: Iran proposed a formal 7-day phased deal on Sept 25 to reopen the Strait of Hormuz — bundling ceasefire, sanctions relief, unfreezing of Iranian assets, and lifting of US naval blockade — in exchange for reopening and nuclear talks resumption. Trump publicly rejected it on Sept 26, calling it 'not acceptable.' However, Trump also told Axios on Sunday (Sept 27) he still expects further talks with intermediaries this week. As of Sept 29, Qatari mediators are holding separate talks with Iran's FM Araghchi and US officials in New York. The strait remains partially open — Kpler data shows Sept crude exports from key Middle Eastern producers rebounding to ~12.8M bpd (highest since war began), but still below pre-conflict levels, keeping market undersupplied per UBS.

Why it matters: Hormuz is the primary transmission mechanism for energy-driven inflation feeding the Fed's hiking impulse. A confirmed reopening deal would: (1) likely take Brent from $105 down 15-20% toward $85-90, removing 2-3 months of inflation pressure; (2) cause the Fed to pause or reduce October hike magnitude, flattening the curve; (3) be powerfully bullish for equities broadly, especially growth/tech; (4) be bearish for TBT (yields drop), BNO, XOM, XLE. This is the single highest-impact binary event in the portfolio's macro environment. It is also time-pressured: Iran's FM returns to Tehran as planned, suggesting a deal window closes or clarifies within days. The asymmetry is stark: deal probability is low (maybe 20-25% this week based on the gap between sides), but the market impact of a deal is enormous.

Bear case: 1) The deal gap is wide: Iran wants nuclear program relief + Oman/Iran co-management of Hormuz; US is demanding nuclear stockpile removal as condition. These are incompatible in a 7-day timeframe. 2) Trump has domestic political incentive to maintain Iran war pressure — backing down is perceived as weakness ahead of 2026 midterms. 3) Iran walked out of Switzerland talks already; pattern suggests brinksmanship rather than genuine deal-seeking. 4) A 'fake deal' that partially reopens then collapses leaves oil only modestly lower, insufficient to shift Fed trajectory. 5) Even with Hormuz open, AI CapEx inflation and tariff-driven core PCE stickiness remain — the Fed's job isn't done.

Why it matters: TBT is the portfolio's macro hedge. AMD data center revenue hit $6.7B in the most recent quarter, up 107% YoY.

Why it matters: The AMD thesis — CPU inference bifurcation where CPUs handle coordination/orchestration for agentic AI while GPUs handle heavy compute — is now supported by hard product and customer data, not just roadmap promises. Venice is real, shipping, and benchmarking well against both Intel and Nvidia's CPU offerings. MI450 has marquee hyperscaler customers.
