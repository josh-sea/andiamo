#1 Jackson Hole: Warsh's First Speech — The Market Is Mispriced Either Way ⚡ 92 MACRO / RATES

One liner: New Fed Chair Kevin Warsh speaks Friday with markets pricing only ~33% odds of a September hike — but PCE is sticky, FOMC minutes showed 'many' members favoring hikes, and Warsh has a hawk pedigree. The asymmetry is striking: if he leans hawkish, bonds and equities both reprice sharply; if he's dovish, a relief rally. Either way, vol is underpriced into the speech.

Key data points:
  • CME FedWatch: 45% December hike, only ~33% September hike
  • July PCE: flat month-over-month, 65th straight month above 2% target
  • FOMC minutes: 'many' officials favor hike absent inflation progress
  • 9-3 dissent vote at July meeting — significant internal division
  • Warsh described as having 'said much less than predecessors' — deliberate opacity adds uncertainty premium

What to watch: Friday Warsh speech tone. Watch for 'data dependent' (dovish read) vs. explicit 'inflation remains too high' framing (hawkish read). Treasury 2Y yield reaction is the cleanest real-time signal.

Bear case on thesis: Warsh may deploy studied ambiguity — giving the market nothing actionable — in which case vol sells off and equities drift higher on relief. His communication style change from predecessors makes him harder to decode.

#2 Copper at Record $6.90/lb — Tariff Front-Running or Real Demand Signal? ⚡ 85 COMMODITY / MACRO

One liner: Copper futures hit ~$6.90/lb, up ~42% globally since 2025, with Southern Copper (SCCO) up 15% in five sessions to $220. The Morningstar/MarketWatch framing is that copper — alongside gold and Bitcoin — is 'sending a message' about U.S. bond market credibility. But the tariff angle complicates the signal: U.S. refined copper imports are running at record pace ahead of potential 15–30% duties from 2027-28, suggesting some of the move is pure front-running arbitrage rather than genuine demand.

Key data points:
  • HG copper futures: ~$6.90/lb, near record
  • SCCO market cap ~$183B — now exceeds Rio Tinto (~$180B)
  • U.S. imported 885K tonnes refined copper H1 2026, annualizing near 2025's record 1.64M tonnes
  • CRU projects 639K tonne global surplus — contradicts price action if tariff front-running is stripped out
  • Morningstar frames copper alongside gold and BTC as a 'bond credibility signal'

What to watch: Separate the two theses: (1) tariff front-running inventory build — check LME/COMEX warehouse stocks; (2) structural green-energy demand. If warehouses are full, the rally is fragile. If they're depleted, it's real. Watch SCCO, FCX, and copper miner spreads.

Bear case on thesis: CRU sees a 639K tonne surplus. If tariff duties are delayed or diluted, the import front-running stops, inventories normalize, and copper retraces hard. Prior tariff-driven copper spikes (2018, 2025) saw mean reversion of 20-35% within 6 months.

#3 Iran War Stagflation Channel: Energy-Driven Inflation Sticking 65 Months Above Target ⚡ 80 GEOPOLITICAL / MACRO

One liner: The U.S.-Iran conflict (six months in, described by Reuters as 'trench warfare stalemate') is running an energy-price shock through the global economy via Strait of Hormuz disruptions. July inflation unexpectedly held steady — the 65th straight month above 2% — and is being explicitly attributed to energy pass-through. This is the critical input into the Warsh/Fed problem above: the Fed can't easily cut (inflation won't cooperate) but hiking into a geopolitical energy shock risks demand destruction. Classic stagflation trap.

Key data points:
  • U.S.-Iran war in its 6th month, stalemate likely into 2027 per Reuters
  • Strait of Hormuz disruptions cited as driver of elevated energy prices
  • July PCE: flat MoM, inflation 'worsened since US and Israel attacked Iran' (Al Jazeera)
  • Fed's Collins: inflation readings 'mixed'; rates 'need to rise soon' absent data improvement
  • Q2 GDP unrevised at 1.5% — weak growth + persistent inflation = textbook stagflation setup

What to watch: Brent crude price action and Hormuz shipping data. If the war de-escalates, energy disinflation could give the Fed room to hold. If it hardens into 2027, stagflation trade (short long bonds, long commodities, long commodity equities) has legs.

Bear case on thesis: The U.S. economy has absorbed oil shocks better than expected in 2022-2024 due to domestic shale production. Energy price effects may be more muted than the narrative suggests. And 1.5% GDP isn't recession — the 'stagflation' label may be premature.
