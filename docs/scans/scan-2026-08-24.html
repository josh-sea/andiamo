#1 Warsh's Jackson Hole Debut — First Rate-Hike Signal in Two Years? ⚡ 92 Macro / Fed Policy

One liner: New Fed Chair Kevin Warsh makes his inaugural Jackson Hole speech Friday Aug 28. FOMC minutes just revealed that after 65 consecutive months of inflation above 2%, policymakers fear businesses are beginning to set wages and prices assuming elevated inflation as the new normal — the textbook precondition for a second-wave inflation spiral.

Why interesting:
  • Three regional Fed presidents already dissented in favor of a July hike — dissenters are growing, not shrinking.
  • July minutes contain the phrase: 'continued elevated inflation rates could begin to affect inflation expectations and wage- and price-setting decisions' — this is not boilerplate language.
  • BofA survey shows RECORD expectations for a 'no landing' scenario among fund managers, meaning consensus is dovish and complacent.
  • Treasury Secretary Bessent simultaneously announced a bond-buying program, which would UNDERCUT Warsh's inflation-fighting credibility if he tries to sound hawkish.
  • 10-year Treasury at 4.69% — long end already pricing some risk.

Bear case: Warsh has historically been hawkish but may choose Jackson Hole to signal patience rather than shock markets. The AI-driven rally gives him cover to wait.

Base rate note: New Fed chairs rarely use their first Jackson Hole to deliver a hawkish shock — Yellen, Powell, and Bernanke all used inaugural speeches to signal continuity. But the macro backdrop here is more hostile than any of those debuts.

Watch for: Any language around 'de-anchoring' of inflation expectations, rate hike path, or criticism of Treasury bond-buying. Yield moves on Friday morning will be the cleaner signal than the speech text itself.

Investable angle: Rate-sensitive names (utilities, REITs, long-duration tech) are the blast radius. Gold could paradoxically rally if Warsh sounds dovish and disappoints hawks.

Base rate note: US Iran sanctions have been reimposed or tightened multiple times since 2018. Each escalation has caused a short oil spike (3–8%) that fades within 3–6 weeks. The only scenario that breaks this pattern is actual Hormuz disruption.

Watch for: Bessent's press conference on sanctions specifics (Aug 24). Whether China (the primary buyer of sanctioned Iranian oil) signals compliance or defiance. Tanker rates as a real-time supply disruption signal.

#5 SPHY vs. SPYD: Credit Cycle Income Divergence as a Macro Thermometer ⚡ 67 Fixed Income / Income Investing / Structural

One liner: Two nearly-identical-looking SPDR income ETFs have diverged by 17 percentage points in 2026 total return (SPYD +15.8% YTD vs. SPHY -1.7%). The mechanism is a signal about where we are in the credit cycle: high-yield bond coupons are resetting LOWER (spreads tight, new issuance cheaper) while dividend equities are being rerated HIGHER.

Why interesting:
  • SPHY's monthly distributions are shrinking year-over-year every single month of 2026 — this is a quiet but consistent signal that the high-yield credit market is not offering the income investors think they're buying.
  • High-yield spreads being tight enough to force coupon resets lower = credit market complacency. This is the same dynamic that precedes spread widening events.
  • With the 10-year at 4.69%, SPHY's risk-adjusted case is hard to defend — you're taking credit risk for LESS than Treasury yield.
  • SPYD's dividend growth (board-discretionary, not contractual) is beating the credit market — this is unusual and suggests equity income is being preferred to credit income at historically tight spreads.
  • Michael Burry reportedly abandoned Alibaba (mentioned in same article) — worth a separate pull.

Bear case: SPYD's 80-stock roster is heavy in energy, pharma, and retail — exactly the sectors exposed to Iran war, tariff shocks, and consumer weakness. The dividend growth could reverse sharply if even a few of those 80 boards cut.

Base rate note: High-yield spread compression has historically been a 6–18 month leading indicator of spread widening events. We're seeing the income signal, not the blowup signal yet.

Watch for: HYG or JNK spread vs. Treasury moving wider. Any SPYD constituent cutting dividends. SPHY distribution trends in the Sept/Oct ex-dates.

Investable angle: Not a trade yet — more a credit cycle positioning signal. If spreads widen (triggered by, say, a hawkish Warsh), SPHY gets worse before it gets better and SPYD's dividend growth stalls. Use as a risk-off canary.
