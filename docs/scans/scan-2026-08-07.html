The Fed September rate hike probability has reportedly slipped below 50% in real-time. The GBP/USD cross already moved above 1.3500 on the print, USD weakened broadly. What happened: Today's NFP shock is the first major data surprise under Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's publicly stated philosophy of minimizing forward guidance and letting bond markets price money. BMO strategist Bipan Rai: 'In a world without forward guidance, incoming data is becoming the primary driver. The market remains unclear about his policy framework and what the Fed's reaction function actually is.'

Why it matters: This is a structural signal about how markets will behave for the next several years, not just today. Warsh's doctrine creates a regime shift: higher sensitivity to each data print, lower anchoring to Fed language, wider implied vol on rates at every FOMC cycle. If the Fed does NOT cut in September despite -23K payrolls (because Warsh prioritizes inflation or credibility), the market will reprice the entire policy function. If they cut, it validates data-dependency and compresses vol. Either way, a 'Warsh regime' means the NFP report is back to being the most important monthly data point in markets — a dynamic that was muted under Yellen/Powell-era guidance. Traders need a new framework.

Key unknowns:
  • Does Warsh speak publicly before September FOMC? His silence would itself be a policy signal.
  • Is inflation still elevated enough to give him cover to hold despite labor contraction?
  • How do Treasury yields react at the long end — does the curve steepen (soft landing trades) or flatten (recession fears)?
  • Historical base rate: How often does a single month of job losses precede recession within 12 months? (Post-WWII: ~60% of the time when followed by a second negative print.)

Watch next: 2-year vs. 10-year Treasury spread; Fed funds futures September implied probability; any Warsh public remarks; next CPI print (Aug) will now carry outsized weight.
