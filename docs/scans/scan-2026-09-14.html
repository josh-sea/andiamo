#1 Fed Hike Into Inflation — The 'Credibility Rally' Setup ⚡ 92 Macro / Rates

What is happening: The Fed under Chairman Kevin Warsh is widely expected to hike to 3.75–4.00% on Wednesday (90% market-implied probability). The 10-year Treasury yield is pressing 5% and the 30-year is at 19-year highs. Trump is publicly demanding the world's lowest rates — Warsh is doing the opposite. The paradox: equity markets may RALLY on the hike if Warsh signals credible inflation commitment, because it anchors the long end of the curve. BofA's Mark Cabana: a hawkish presser = 2Y +5–10bps, 30Y -5–10bps (bull steepener in equities' favor). A dovish presser = inverse, and bond chaos.

Why interesting: This is a rare 'good hike' setup. The market is not fighting the Fed — it WANTS the hike as a credibility signal. That is structurally unusual and historically uncommon. Canaccord base rate: S&P 500 averages -3.4% in the month after first hike. But this is not a first hike in a normal cycle — this is a mid-cycle re-hike into re-accelerating inflation, with fiscal deficits at post-COVID highs. The Warsh press conference tone is the entire trade.

Key variables:
  • Warsh press conference tone (hawkish vs. dovish)
  • 30Y yield reaction on Wednesday
  • October/December hike probability re-pricing in fed funds futures
  • August CPI already reported in-line — does Warsh treat as confirmation or insufficient?

#3 Copper — Tariff Whipsaw Creates Entry Point in Structural Bull ⚡ 79 Commodities / Industrials

What is happening: A 15% US tariff on refined copper imports was expected — traders panic-bought copper to ship into the US ahead of it, pushing LME prices above $14,300/metric ton (all-time record). Then: the White House paused the tariff out of fear it would spike manufacturing costs and worsen inflation (the very Fed problem above). The copper price has crashed sharply on profit-taking and speculative unwinding. Physical demand is unaffected. Structural drivers — AI data centers, EV charging, grid upgrades — remain intact. Resource nationalism (Chile 46.5% effective tax rate, Indonesia export bans) is fragmenting global supply.

Why interesting: Policy-driven sell-offs in structurally constrained commodities are classic entry signal candidates — but timing is brutal. The tariff pause removes the most acute near-term pressure, but re-introduction risk is high and will create future volatility. The long-duration thesis (resource nationalism + beneficiation deglobalization + AI power demand) is structurally sound. The question is whether the current selloff overshoots fair value enough to be actionable.

Key variables:

  • Chile mining royalty enforcement trajectory
  • US manufacturing PMI — does copper tariff pause reduce input cost fears?
  • Data center copper demand growth rate (projected to 2x)
Red flags:

  • China demand outlook uncertain amid its own policy pivots
  • Tariff policy is currently unpredictable — single announcement can move prices 10%+ in a session
